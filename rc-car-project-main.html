Result Designed and built a fully custom RC car from scratch — chassis in Fusion 360, electronics on breakout boards, wireless control via ESP-NOW. Fully assembled and driving; custom PCB in progress.

Started this because I couldn't find an RC car kit that didn't feel like a toy. Designed the chassis in Fusion 360, printed everything in PLA — including the wheels. ESP-NOW ended up being the right wireless choice once I fixed a channel mismatch that was causing complete dropouts. The current build runs on breakout boards and jumper wires; a custom PCB is in progress — started in EasyEDA, now migrated to KiCad — targeting JLCPCB manufacture in the coming weeks.

What I Learned
  • ESP-NOW requires both devices on the same WiFi channel — there's no error message when it's wrong, just silence. Took me embarrassingly long to find in the docs.
  • Nobody tells you joystick ADCs are noisy. Without dead zones (~8% on each axis) the car creeps and twitches at rest. Spent an afternoon thinking the motor driver was faulty.
  • LEDC PWM in Arduino Core v3+ needs explicit channel assignment. The old analogWrite() approach silently stopped working — nowhere obvious in the changelog. Cost me a full day.
  • The XIAO ESP32-C3's built-in LiPo connector and onboard 3.3 V regulator eliminated an entire buck converter compared to v1. Battery connects directly to the XIAO, motor module, and servo — three connections, no conversion stage, one less thing to fail.

A custom-designed, 3D-printed RC car — including the wheels — built around the Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C3. The chassis was modelled from scratch in Fusion 360 and printed on a Creality Ender-3 S1 Pro. Drive is handled by a DC 130 dual-shaft motor (3V–6V, 12,000 RPM, direct drive) controlled via a TB6612FNG H-bridge module, while steering uses a Surpass Hobby micro digital servo (13.5 g, plastic gear) for Ackermann-style front-wheel control.

Wireless control runs over ESP-NOW between two ESP32-C3 boards — one on the car (receiver) and one on a breadboard transmitter: an ESP32-C3 SuperMini with two KY-023 analog joystick modules. Motor speed is controlled with LEDC PWM and joystick dead zones to keep low-speed movement stable.

The XIAO's built-in LiPo connector and onboard 3.3 V regulator eliminates the need for an external buck converter — the 3.7 V battery connects directly to the XIAO, motor module, and servo. A custom PCB is in progress to replace the current protoboard wiring — targeting JLCPCB manufacture in the coming weeks.

CAD Design & 3D Printing

The full chassis was designed parametrically in Fusion 360 as a multi-body assembly: main body, front steering knuckles, wheel mounts, motor bracket, servo bracket, and the wheels themselves — all 3D printed. Every component was designed to fit the micro digital servo and the DC 130 motor directly.

Phase 1 — Fusion 360 Design
Full chassis assembly with 4 wheels, motor mount, servo bracket, and electronics bay
The chassis was designed as a complete parametric assembly in Fusion 360. Components include: main chassis plate, front axle steering knuckles for Ackermann geometry, rear motor bracket sized to the DC 130 dual-shaft motor, servo bracket with horn clearance, four wheel mounts, and a top electronics bay for the PCB and LiPo cell. The wheels were also designed and printed, keeping the entire build self-made. All tolerances were set to account for FDM dimensional variation on the Ender-3 S1 Pro.
Phase 2 — 3D Printing on Ender-3 S1 Pro
PLA print with focus on front steering section and wheel cutout geometry
All chassis parts printed in PLA on the Creality Ender-3 S1 Pro. The front steering section with wheel cutouts required careful orientation to print without supports while maintaining dimensional accuracy for the servo horn interface. Layer height set to 0.2 mm for structural parts, 0.15 mm for steering knuckles requiring tighter tolerances. Parts were test-fitted before electronics installation.

Electronics & PCB Design

The electronics stack is built around the XIAO ESP32-C3 paired with a TB6612FNG H-bridge module. Supporting circuitry sits on a small protoboard: a power switch, red LED indicator, electrolytic capacitor for motor voltage spike suppression, and battery and motor connectors. A 100 nF ceramic capacitor is soldered across the motor terminals for brush noise suppression. A custom PCB replacing all of this is in progress in KiCad — the schematic was started in EasyEDA before being migrated to KiCad — targeting JLCPCB manufacture in the coming weeks.

Firmware

Written in Arduino C++ using the ESP-NOW API and the LEDC driver directly. The receiver auto-calibrates joystick centres on power-up (~60 packets at 50 Hz) and requires the throttle to sit at centre briefly before arming — preventing a motor spin on connect. Motor PWM runs at 20 kHz on an 11-bit timer; servo outputs 50 Hz with pulse width constrained to 1000–2000 µs. A 400 ms failsafe cuts the motor if packets stop arriving.

Build & Assembly

Phase 1 — Bench Testing
Motor, servo, and driver validated independently before chassis mounting
Before final assembly, each subsystem was bench-tested independently. The TB6612FNG motor driver was tested for bidirectional PWM control with the XIAO ESP32-C3 using both LEDC channels. The micro digital servo was validated for full range of motion on GPIO D7. Motor direction switching and speed control were verified across the full PWM range. All bench tests ran from LiPo power through a bench PSU current limiter for safety.
Phase 2 — Chassis Assembly
3D-printed chassis assembled with motor, servo, and wheel mounts
All 3D-printed chassis parts assembled in sequence: rear motor bracket press-fitted and secured, front steering knuckles inserted with press-fit axle pins, micro digital servo mounted in its bracket with horn connected to the steering linkage, and all four wheels attached. Fit and full steering range were checked before installing any electronics.
Phase 3 — Electronics Mounting
XIAO ESP32-C3, TB6612FNG breakout, and LiPo mounted to chassis top plate
Electronics mounted to the chassis top plate. XIAO ESP32-C3 positioned for USB-C access, with its antenna sticker adhered to the chassis exterior for unobstructed RF. TB6612FNG module wired to all motor and GPIO pins per pin mapping table. LiPo cell positioned for centre-of-gravity balance. Wireless control tested end-to-end with the ESP-NOW transmitter — car drives and steers correctly in both directions.

Next Steps

PCB Design, Manufacture & Testing — JLCPCB
Complete the KiCad PCB design and submit to JLCPCB for 2-layer fabrication. Assemble the custom PCB and validate all subsystems: motor driver PWM, servo control, power supply, and ESP-NOW wireless link. Replace the current breakout-board wiring with the clean PCB-mounted solution.
Handheld Transmitter Controller Build
Design a dedicated handheld transmitter around a XIAO ESP32-C3 with the ESP-NOW TX firmware. The controller will feature a dual-axis analog joystick for throttle and steering, housed in a 3D-printed ergonomic enclosure designed in Fusion 360.
Body Shell — Fusion 360 & 3D Print
Design a full body shell enclosure in Fusion 360 that mounts over the electronics chassis — styled to look like a scaled car body. The shell clips onto the chassis top plate and includes ventilation slots for the motor and power electronics. Printed in PLA with optional colour accent pieces.
PWM & Dead Zone Tuning
Dial in the right PWM frequency and dead zone parameters for low-speed control precision and reliable direction switching. Current firmware uses hardcoded dead zones — a runtime tuning mode via the joystick will make it possible to dial these in on the fly and save them to EEPROM.
